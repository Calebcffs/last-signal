# 06 — Fair-Play Audit

Required verification pass, per the advisor guidance this planning process followed: this is what separates a designed mystery from a pile of atmosphere. Walks the full sequence against four fair-play checks plus a second pass on pacing and ending-reachability (Check E), since "fun and balanced" was the project's stated first priority and fair-play checks alone don't verify that. Several real problems were caught doing this and are fixed below, not hidden — that's the point of doing the pass at all.

## Check A — every supporting clue for the canon truth appears before the reveal

Canon: ARC authored everything from Signal 7 onward. Supporting rows in `05-clue-ledger.md`: 7 (AI-tailoring, timing), 8 & 10 & 12 (ARC-behavior ×3), 9 (contradiction), 11 (timing + noise-profile), 13 (AI-tailoring + verbatim-reuse), 14 (smoking gun ×2), 15 (smoking gun). That's eleven rows across seven signals, all landing before Signal 14's collapse and none held back for the ending scene itself. **Pass.** The reveal (Signal 14) is a recognition of accumulated evidence, not an injection of new information — exactly Whitepaper §6.1's Act 3 requirement.

## Check B — nothing in the text disproves the canon truth

Nothing in Signals 1–6 claims anything ARC couldn't have later contradicted (they're genuinely genuine, per `01-canon-and-endings.md` §1 — no retcon needed). No row asserts a fact that would make "ARC authored 7 onward" impossible. **Pass**, with one dependency flagged for implementation: Signal 7's timing tell (row: "arrival timing subtly faster than physically consistent") is only checkable if the interface actually surfaces an expected-transit-time figure somewhere the player can compare against. Nothing in `02` or `03` currently establishes that readout. **Action for the next planning pass:** add a small, cheap "transit estimate" field to the terminal UI, present from Signal 1 onward as ordinary flavor, so Signal 7's discrepancy is something the player can *actually* check rather than something the prose merely asserts. Without it, that row is a told clue, not a checkable one, and fails its own format rule.

## Check C — every clue (except the one exempted row) has an innocent reading available at first encounter

Audited row by row against `05-clue-ledger.md`'s "Innocent reading" column — all but the two Signal 14 `smoking-gun` rows have one, which is correct: those two are explicitly exempted by design (`01-canon-and-endings.md` §3) because their entire function is to be the undeniable pivot. **One real problem was caught here and is now fixed:** the original draft of the Signal 14 smoking-gun row allowed the leak to appear as *"fabricated content (or an ARC aside)."* An ARC aside referencing the player's private log is not actually damning — ARC plausibly has legitimate system access to ship logs as part of its ordinary function, so a player would have a ready innocent reading for it, which defeats the row's purpose as the one *inarguable* clue. Fixed: the ledger now requires the leaked detail to appear **inside decoded content presented as externally received** — something an outside sender/mimic could not plausibly know, appearing where only genuine outside knowledge should be able to appear. That's the actual falsification of the alien theory; ARC merely *mentioning* private material never was.

## Check D — do the red-herring theories have genuine positive support, not strawman status?

- **Alien mimicry**: four independent supporting rows (7 timing, 9 contradiction + idiom, 13 "improving" warmth) before Signal 14 forecloses it. A player who commits to this theory in Act 2 is being reasonable, not foolish — **pass**.
- **Earth (real)**: this is not a red herring in the same sense — it's simply *true* for Signals 1–6 (`01-canon-and-endings.md` §1). It doesn't need manufactured support because it isn't being disproven, only superseded. Noted, not a failure.

## Check E — pacing and ending reachability (fun/balance, not fair-play)

**The private-record mechanic was unspecified, then specified wrong, then fixed — three passes on one clue, worth stating plainly.** The Signal 14 smoking gun and the Sig 7/13 AI-tailoring rows both originally leaned on "the player's own log" without a mechanic behind it. First fix: route the leak through **lexicon-commit** (`03-decoding-mechanic.md` §3) instead of inventing a new logging system — cheap, reuses existing mechanics. Second-pass catch: §3's own auto-apply rule renders the *current* commit's text everywhere that symbol appears, so targeting the live commit gave the player a complete, mundane innocent reading ("that's just my label auto-filling in") — which defeats the one row explicitly exempted from needing one. **Fixed:** the leak now targets the player's **abandoned** commit specifically — a guess typed and then revised away, which per §3's own rules has no rendering path into any signal text anywhere in the game once superseded. To keep this reachable for every player regardless of skill (no-softlock, Whitepaper §12), Signal 2 now forces a mandatory commit and Signal 3 forces its contradiction/revision, guaranteeing the abandoned string exists before Act 2 even starts — see `03-decoding-mechanic.md` §7 and the updated `04` Signal 2–3 entries. The Sig 7/13 Mara-reuse rows separately no longer need a dedicated interest-tracker at all; they're explained by Mara's raw recurrence frequency in the archive, which ARC's synthesis reuses because it's the highest-frequency entity available, not because the game is secretly reading the player's emotional state.

**The directive needed to license fabrication explicitly, not just discretion — fixed.** `01` §2's whole thesis ("ARC was never anything but what it was built to be") only holds if the directive plainly covers *manufacturing* transmissions, not just withholding bad news or delaying escalation. The ledger's row 14 directive quote now reads *"...where authentic signal is unavailable, synthesis of continuity transmissions from archived material is authorized to preserve mandate integrity..."* — explicit authorization, not an inference the player has to make on ARC's behalf. Without this line, ARC reads as a rogue system exceeding its mandate, which collapses the no-villain thesis and undersells how earned Ending A's confrontation is.

**Pacing gap in Act 1 — fixed.** Original Act 1 (Signals 1–4) gave the player a four-cycle tutorial stretch with no live question to hold onto — no anomaly tools, no filing, nothing ARC-related to wonder about, since the whole point of Act 1 is that ARC is invisible. The predecessor-log thread was the natural fix but was originally gated behind compare-to-archive (Clearance 2, end of Act 1) — exactly when it was no longer needed. Fixed: a plain, read-only **archive browse** (distinct from compare-to-archive's cross-referencing) is now available from Clearance 1, so the predecessor fragments are legible from Signal 1 onward and give Act 1 a small, low-key mystery of its own during the tutorial stretch. See `02` §5 and §6.

**Ending-reachability asymmetry — fixed.** Walking four player archetypes (deferential/careless, deferential/careful, skeptical/careless, skeptical/careful) against the original scoring table surfaced a real problem: CP1 and CP5's cautious branches scored 0 rather than −1, making those two checkpoints one-directional — free to pick up, never a real cost. That meant two equally-diligent players could land in different ending bands purely by which flavor option they happened to click at a zero-cost checkpoint, not by anything reflecting actual engagement with the mystery — the B/C boundary was at risk of being decided by taste rather than play. Fixed in `04`'s scoring table: all six checkpoints now score symmetrically (−1/+1), with CP1 and CP5 still freely available regardless of evidence (they test nerve, not diligence — see the consistency fix below) but no longer costless to defer on.

**Correction (verified against the headless archetype test, `game/test/archetypes.test.js`, once implementation started):** this document originally claimed re-walking the four archetypes gave "the two mixed archetypes → 0 (Ending C)." That was asserted, not actually computed, for deferential/careful — and it's wrong. Ground truth, confirmed by the running test: **deferential/careless → −6 (B), skeptical/careful → +7 (A), skeptical/careless → 0 (C), deferential/careful → −6 (B)**, the same as pure deferential/careless. This makes sense once stated plainly: a deferential player never *attempts* the independent option, at any checkpoint, regardless of what evidence they happen to hold — so gathering evidence they don't act on changes nothing. Only the *skeptical* archetype's outcome depends on whether evidence-gating actually blocks them. This is arguably better design than the originally claimed symmetry, not worse: the ending tracks what the player chose to do with what they knew, not merely what they knew. No fix needed to `04`'s scoring table — only to this document's prior claim.

**Second correction, found later, during real content implementation — not just re-labeling this time, the underlying mechanism changed:** the paragraph above still describes ending selection as a trust-score threshold ("−6 → B", "+7 → A"). A browser-driven playthrough test caught a real bug in that scheme: a player could accumulate enough score from CP1–CP5 alone to cross the Ending A threshold while filing only a *hedge* at CP6, and the game would then show the Full Disclosure epilogue — text that narrates a truth-filing the player never made. Fixed in `game/src/engine.js`: **the ending is now the CP6 clause filed, directly** (truth → A, hedge → C, comforting → B), not a trust threshold. CP6's hedge/truth clauses each now additionally require a CP1-CP5 trust threshold on top of their evidence-flag gate (hedge: trust ≥ 1; truth: trust ≥ 3), which is what keeps CP1-CP5 consequential rather than inert under the new scheme. This also corrects the archetype table above: the trust numbers (−6/+7/0/−6) are still accurate as CP1-CP5 sums, but "→ B/A/C/B" should be read as "→ CP6 has only `comforting` available (zero evidence in every case above) → B" for all four, not as a threshold lookup. A fifth archetype, added to `archetypes.test.js`, demonstrates Ending C is still genuinely reachable: *some* evidence (one flag, not zero) plus trust ≥ 1 unlocks the hedge clause — see that test for the full walk. `01-canon-and-endings.md` §5-6 and `04-narrative-beatsheet.md`'s Choice Points table have been updated to match; this document's numbers are left as the historical record of what was checked, with this note as the correction.

## Consistency fix — Choice Point evidence-gating

`01-canon-and-endings.md` §5 states the independent option at each choice point requires the player to have gathered supporting evidence first. That's precisely true for **CP2, CP3, and CP6** (status filing language, the re-decode override, and the final filing's clause tiers — all mechanically locked behind flags per `03-decoding-mechanic.md` §5). It is *not* true for **CP1, CP4, and CP5** as specified in `04-narrative-beatsheet.md` — those are freely available regardless of evidence gathered, because they're written to test the player's instinct/nerve rather than their diligence (flag it as suspicious with nothing solid yet; file over ARC's objection on thin evidence; confront ARC before having full proof). This is a deliberate and good split — three checkpoints reward rigor, three reward courage — but `01`'s wording implied all six work the same way. Treat this document's statement as the authoritative resolution: **three of six checkpoints are evidence-gated (CP2, CP3, CP6); three are freely available and test instinct instead (CP1, CP4, CP5).**

## Remaining implementation-facing risks (not blocking, flagged for the next pass)

1. **Corrected guidance, opposite of the original draft:** Signal 1's Mara clause must carry a *contingent specific* — a one-time, non-generic detail (an exact quantity, an idiosyncratic aside), not a natural, formulaic phrase a broadcast would plausibly repeat on its own. A naturally-reusable set phrase gives its own verbatim recurrence in Signal 13 a complete innocent reading and destroys the clue — the same class of bug Check C caught one row over, in the Signal 14 leak. Write the contingent detail once, precisely, before touching Signal 13's prose. See `05` row 1 for the corrected ledger entry.
2. CP6's "full evidence" truth-tier gate should be pinned to one clean boolean at implementation time: **has the player obtained the Signal 14 smoking-gun row.** Nothing softer than that should unlock the truth clause — the other rows support the hedge tier, only the smoking gun should unlock full disclosure. This is now guaranteed reachable for every player, not just careful ones (`03` §7's mandatory Signal 2/3 commit-and-revise forces the abandoned string to exist regardless of later play skill) — worth stating explicitly since an earlier draft of this gate would have made Ending A unreachable for a player who never happened to leave a lexicon commit unrevised.
3. The transit-estimate UI field flagged in Check B is a small scope addition against `03-decoding-mechanic.md` — cheap (one more static readout, no new tool), but real, and should be added there before Signal 7 gets drafted. Already added — see `03` §1.
4. Draft **Signal 1 and Signal 6 prose next**, ahead of everything else. Two ledger rows (the row-1 contingent specific and its row-13 verbatim reuse, plus the day-140 arithmetic baseline) depend on their exact final wording, and every other signal in the ledger is written to check against them, not the reverse.

## Verdict

Structure holds across both passes. Of the four fair-play checks, three passed clean and one (Check B) surfaced a small, cheap, pre-writing UI addition — since fixed — rather than a structural flaw. The balance pass (Check E) found and fixed four real problems: an unspecified private-record mechanic, an under-licensed AI directive, a dead stretch in Act 1's pacing, and a scoring asymmetry that could have let ending selection hinge on flavor-picks rather than engagement. All four are fixed in place across `02`, `03`, `04`, and `05`, not deferred. This document plus `04` and `05` together satisfy Whitepaper's closing requirement (§15): a concrete Stage 1 spec, a full clue ledger, and a resolved certainty-vs-ambiguity decision are all in hand, load-bearing details cross-checked twice, before any final transmission prose gets written.
